## 1. Core job and method

When I have shortlisted two or three plausible homes, I want to understand relevant historical transactions, property differences and evidence limitations, so I can decide which alternatives merit further consideration and which questions remain unanswered.

This is an authored job statement, not a buyer quotation. It describes progress toward a decision rather than a feature or a prediction. Applies to HPI-01 and overlapping unfamiliar-market HPI-02; the latter may need more explanation of geographic relevance. First-time status is not an eligibility rule. Existing scope and prior decisions (S), published population-level evidence (E), and hypothesised jobs/outcomes (H) are distinct. No measured importance, frequency, satisfaction, willingness to pay or opportunity scores exist for these jobs. Prioritise qualitatively by necessity to the bounded buyer workflow, evidence relevance, scope fit and risk if omitted, not by invented customer rankings.

## 3. Evidence and opposing hypotheses

A.2 S1 (NAR 2026 successful US primary-residence buyers) reports 37% very useful and 41% somewhat useful recent-sale details; this supports relevance of JT2, not unmet need or local demand. The same study reports 90% agent-assisted purchases and 92% satisfaction with buying, important counterevidence to JT4/JT5/JT8 unmet-need assumptions. Its low response rate, retrospective and successful-buyer selection limit extrapolation. A.2 S2 reports some decision doubts and price regret, but also high purchase satisfaction; no objective overpayment or emotional benefit established. A.3 S3 historical Miami-Dade condo study suggests an unfamiliar-market information-gap mechanism, but not transferable effect size or local behaviour. A.1 records different price concepts, exceptional transfers and publication delays. Source freshness is a prerequisite to decision relevance; current retrospective 2024-2025 sales / 2023-2024 assessments do not establish contemporary or historical-as-of coverage.

Disconfirming conditions: an agent or portal already provides sufficient comprehensible current evidence; buyer wants affordability or live listings rather than historical comparison; data too stale, narrow, ambiguous or legally unavailable; entry and organisation effort exceeds benefit; or buyers incorrectly infer a recommended price. If any critical condition dominates, HPI may not be useful for the intended scenario. Do not infer competitive differentiation before P0-B. Do not pivot to an investor or professional market to bypass these conditions.

## 5. Validation plan and stopping criterion

Later approved task assessment should first ask how a buyer handled the last shortlist decision and what existing alternatives they used; then present matched fictional or expressly authorised scenarios covering sufficient, exploratory, missing and stale evidence. Observe whether JT1-JT4/JT6 outcomes can be achieved, whether JT5 actually saves effort, and whether JT7/JT8 arise without leading questions. Compare with current workflows using the same decision information; measure correctness, comprehension, appropriate abstention and task effort, rather than only self-rated confidence. Separate unmet need from usability and actual adoption from stated intent. Do not use actual price savings, conversion, anxiety reduction or purchase success as claimed results. A.9 will propose success measures and thresholds; A.7 maps job/pain to the candidate value proposition. No recruitment, evaluation or contact authorised here.
