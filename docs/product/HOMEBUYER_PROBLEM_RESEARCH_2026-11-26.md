# P0-A.2 | US homebuyer problem research

**Research date:** 26 November 2026. **Status:** bounded secondary research verified against the P0-A.2 stopping criterion; not direct product-demand validation, market acceptance or phase closure. **Authority:** [P0 directive](../engineering/P0_DISCOVERY_AND_FEASIBILITY_DIRECTIVE.md), original P0.1/P0.2/P0.6. Existing [industry research](RESIDENTIAL_PROPERTY_INDUSTRY_RESEARCH_2026-11-26.md), [product brief](PRODUCT_BRIEF.md) and [pinnacle vision](PINNACLE_VISION.md) govern scope. US primary-residence buyers are the focus; no local Evanston survey or new property extraction.

## 1. Research question and hypotheses

Do buyers experience decision problems that explain a need for a transparent historical evidence and shortlist workspace? Separate the size of a general housing problem, evidence of a specific pain, unmet need after existing alternatives, and demonstrated HPI benefit. These are different claims.

H1 difficulty assessing alternatives; H2 uncertainty about asking price; H3 unequal access to market knowledge; H4 fragmentation of research; H5 pressure or emotional influence; H6 unmet need beyond current assistance. Seek counterevidence, not only support.

## 3. Findings and opposing evidence

**S1 (national survey):** 56% cited difficulty finding a suitable property; first-time/repeat rates 55%/57%. Understanding the process: 39%/8%. These categories overlap. Search median was ten weeks. Recent-sale details on property sites received 37% very-useful and 41% somewhat-useful ratings (Exhibit 3-8); this is direct evidence of relevance, not that existing features are inadequate. Agents assisted 90% of purchases, and 92% of buyers were satisfied with the process. The report connects search difficulty with supply and affordability; neither is solved by HPI. The data support an adjunct rather than an agent/portal replacement.

**S2 (commercial survey):** 45% experienced decision doubts, 42% reported moving sooner than comfortable, 21% regretted their price and 20% regretted limited negotiation. Conversely, 71% would repeat the purchase decision and 81% were happy with it. Self-reported regret is not evidence of overpayment relative to a counterfactual market price. Different respondents and survey wording prevent aggregation with S1 or S4.

**S3 (historical academic evidence):** the study reports a nonlocal purchase premium consistent with search and anchoring mechanisms; the search-cost component diminished modestly over time. Supports a plausible information-gap mechanism, while also indicating that improved online information can reduce it. No estimate transferable to Cook County Class 203 or HPI effectiveness.

**S4 (lower-confidence counterpoint):** reported perceived overpayment was 15% in 2023 and 8% in 2025. Market conditions and differing designs matter. Do not treat this as contradictory to S2's 21% in a statistical sense or average them. It challenges a universal or stable price-regret claim.

**S5 (directional):** 92% reportedly used an online shopping resource. Existing digital tools are already widely used. This alone cannot show fragmentation, difficulty interpreting comparables, or willingness to adopt another workspace.

## 4. Hypothesis assessment

| Hypothesis | Evidence judgement | Consequence for product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 evaluating alternatives | Broader search problem supported; shortlist-specific difficulty unmeasured | Preserve bounded comparison; do not claim to solve inventory/affordability |
| H2 asking-price uncertainty | Some indirect support from doubts/regret; direct recent-sale relevance from S1; objective overpayment unproven | Show provenance, earlier screened sales, differences and uncertainty; no fair-price verdict or guaranteed savings |
| H3 unequal market knowledge | Historical evidence supports plausible mechanism; local size/current prevalence unknown | Explain evidence plainly without assuming all buyers or agents lack knowledge |
| H4 fragmented research | Multiple channels observed; actual duplication, switching costs and manual comparison burden not measured | Saved workspace is a plausible response, not a proven unmet need |
| H5 emotional/time pressure | Self-report supports some buyer pressure; market supply/financing may constrain choices; most still satisfied | Keep comparison accessible, clear and non-coercive; never claim to remove anxiety or prevent regrets |
| H6 unmet need beyond alternatives | Not established; substantial existing agent and portal use and high satisfaction | Test incremental value against actual current alternatives; no claim of unique capability or market demand |

## 5. Existing alternatives and scope of the opportunity

Observed channels: agents and online property resources. Other plausible workarounds from P0-A.1 include assessor records and personal notes/spreadsheets; usage frequency and pain not observed in this research. Agent advice, current listings, professional appraisal and inspection may be sufficient for many buyers. No comparative product benchmark yet; P0-B will test overlap and differentiation.

Priority problem candidate (research inference): **a buyer who already has viable shortlisted homes may benefit from putting eligible historical sales, differences and limitations together in a traceable decision record.** Do not frame it as discovering the right house, predicting its value, resolving affordability or replacing professional judgement. Historical data lag and property/market boundaries make contemporary offer-price claims especially unsafe. Validate whether the evidence adds value even when actual current-market data is available elsewhere; stale or insufficient coverage could make the product unsuitable for a present-day buyer.

## 6. Research gaps and next evidence

- Direct interviews/task observations in target market not performed; no measured willingness to adopt, pay, switch or use HPI, no direct comparative interpretation difficulty rate.
- Secondary surveys overrepresent successful purchases; local and unsuccessful buyer needs remain uncertain. No local extrapolation or causal claim.
- P0-A.3 will evaluate segments (including first-time, repeat, unfamiliar-market buyers, investors and professionals) against need, reachable scope, evidence fit, current alternatives and risks. No demographic persona asserted yet.
- Later P0-A.5-A.9/P0-B should test shortlist evidence jobs and existing workflows; propose a controlled task assessment measuring evidence comprehension, correct provenance/limitations, effort and appropriate abstention, not claimed savings. Actual participant recruitment/contact remains subject to approval.
- P0-C/D retain data freshness, identity and rights gates; no raw data, legal permission, market choice or new feature implied.
